# Audit Hub basics

## Where things are in the Audit Hub
Open the left menu, choose Security and Compliance, then Audit Hub. The Audit Hub has these pages, and you only see the ones your role allows:
- Audits: every audit you can see, with its framework, product and progress. The New Audit button is on this page for people who may create audits.
- Dashboard: audit progress at a glance, your work queue, and progress by team.
Click an audit to open it. Inside an audit you see its controls in a table; click a control to open its details panel. Each audit also has an activity log for internal users. The User Guide link is at the bottom of the left menu. The round Ask AI button at the bottom right of every Audit Hub page opens the Audit assistant, which answers questions and shows numbers, lists and charts for the audits, controls and evidence you have access to.

## How to find a control
Open Audit Hub, then Audits, and click the audit. The controls are listed in a table; use the filters above it to narrow by status, team or owner. Click a control to open its details panel. You can also ask the assistant to take you there, for example: take me to CA-01 in HIPAA GRC Platform 2027.

## What is in a control's details panel
The panel has three tabs: Overview, Evidence and History. Overview shows the control's requirement, type, scope, due date, owner, team and auditor. Evidence is where evidence is submitted and reviewed. History is the control's timeline of who did what and when, for internal users.

## How to submit evidence
1. Open Audit Hub, then Audits, and click the audit that has your control.
2. Click your control to open its details panel and go to the Evidence tab.
3. Add your files, then click Submit Evidence. You can instead write a short note explaining why there are no files, if that applies.
4. The evidence then goes to internal review. If it is sent back, the control shows what to fix and you can resubmit.
You only see the submit button for controls you own, and only when the control is waiting for your evidence.

## How to submit a population
Some controls (operating effectiveness) start with a population. Open the control, and in the Evidence tab click Submit Population (or Resubmit Population if it was sent back). Once the population is approved the auditor selects a sample, and then you submit the evidence for that sample.

## How evidence is reviewed
A control moves through these steps. Operating effectiveness controls: Submit Population, Sample Selection, Submit Evidence, Review. Design controls: Submit Evidence, then Review. Reviewers see Approve and Reject buttons on submitted work, and a rejection comes back to the owner with a comment. The control's status in the table shows where it is, for example Evidence Pending, Evidence Internal Review, Evidence Under Validation, Evidence Need Clarification, or Complete.

## What the statuses mean
- Population Pending, Population Internal Review, Population Under Validation, Population Need Clarification, Population Complete: the population phase of an operating effectiveness control.
- Awaiting Sample and Submitted Sample: the auditor is choosing, or has chosen, the sample.
- Evidence Pending: waiting for the owner to submit evidence.
- Evidence Internal Review: submitted and being checked inside the company.
- Evidence Under Validation: with the auditor.
- Evidence Need Clarification: sent back with a question.
- Complete: finished.
A control is overdue when it is past its due date and not Complete.

## How to create an audit
Open Audit Hub, then Audits, and click New Audit. Choose the framework and product, name the audit and set its period, then create it. The controls come from the framework. If you do not see New Audit, your role does not allow creating audits.

## Who does what
- Control owners submit populations and evidence for the controls they own.
- Internal reviewers check submissions and approve them or send them back.
- Auditors select samples and validate evidence. They only see the controls they audit.
- Audit managers see all audits, or the audits of the teams they manage.
External auditors use a separate, limited view and do not have the assistant.
